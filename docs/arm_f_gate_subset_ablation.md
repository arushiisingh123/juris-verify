# D1 — Gate-subset ablation (arm F adjudicator)

Analysis only. No production file was modified, no model was run, no threshold
or gate was introduced. Every number below is recomputed from gate outcomes
already stored in the arm F artifact.

Generated by `inspect_gate_subset_ablation.py`; raw figures in
`docs/arm_f_gate_subset_ablation_data.json`.

---

## A. Experimental setup

- **Artifact:** `eval/results_20261009_085502_n504_section2_w0.05_prosactive.json`
  (504 claims, `evidence_mode=section`, `max_sentences=2`, `section_weight=0.05`,
  `prosecution_mode=active`, 217.5 s runtime)
- **Arm D reference:** 421/504 correct (83.53%); CONTRADICT 115/173 (66.47%);
  SUPPORT 306/331 (92.45%) — read from the artifact's own `baseline_verdict`
  field, not assumed.
- **Method:** for each of the 16 subsets `S ⊆ {G1,G2,G3,G4}`, recompute the
  counterfactual override set as

  ```
  override(S) = has_counter_evidence
                AND every gate in S passes
                AND baseline_prediction == "SUPPORT"
  ```

  then rescore. `baseline_prediction == "SUPPORT"` is exactly production's
  `primary_verdict != CONTRADICTED` precondition, so baseline-CONTRADICT claims
  are never downgraded in any subset — matching arm F.

- **No model was invoked.** `src.prosecution.adjudicate` computes all four gates
  *unconditionally*, before the first-failure logic picks which to report as the
  reason, so the stored `adjudication_gates` dict carries every gate's outcome.

## B. Gate definitions

Let C = claim, P = primary (baseline) premise, X = counter-evidence.

| gate | test | intent |
|---|---|---|
| **G1** symmetry | `verdict(premise=C, hypothesis=X) == CONTRADICTED` | a real contradiction holds in both directions; a merely topical sentence conflicts one way only |
| **G2** coherence | `verdict(premise=P, hypothesis=X) != CONTRADICTED` | if the premise refutes the counter, the two retrieved sentences disagree with each other, not with the claim |
| **G3** uncontested support | `primary_verdict != SUPPORTED` | if the source entails the claim, a contradictory fragment elsewhere is not grounds to flip the label |
| **G4** finding-grounded | counter section ∈ {results, conclusions}, **or** (section unrecoverable **and** counter from the document the baseline cited) | Background/Methods report prior work or design, not this study's finding |

## G. Interpretation — what G4 is actually doing

Two structural facts explain G4's behaviour.

**1. G4 operates mostly through its fallback, not its section rule.** Of the 289
counter-evidence sentences, **199 (68.9%) have `counter_section == "unknown"`**:

| counter section | n (of 289) | share |
|---|---|---|
| unknown | 199 | 68.9% |
| results | 38 | 13.1% |
| conclusions | 34 | 11.8% |
| methods | 9 | 3.1% |
| background | 9 | 3.1% |

So for ~69% of cases G4 is not a section test at all — it is a *same-document*
provenance test. The gate I designed as section-based is in practice
provenance-based. (Only 11 of the 112 overridable unknown-counter claims sit in
documents where arm D's own spans carried a real section, so this is mostly
genuinely unstructured abstracts rather than heuristic failure.)

**2. The section ordering G4 assumes is only partly borne out.** Ceiling
precision by section over the full 150 overridable claims (base rate 29.33%):

| counter section | n | rescuable | losable | ceiling precision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| conclusions | 10 | 5 | 5 | **50.0%** |
| unknown | 112 | 33 | 79 | 29.5% |
| results | 15 | 4 | 11 | **26.7%** |
| methods | 6 | 1 | 5 | 16.7% |
| background | 7 | 1 | 6 | 14.3% |

`conclusions` clearly beats the base rate and `methods`/`background` clearly
trail it — both as designed. But **`results` (26.7%) does not beat `unknown`
(29.5%) or even the base rate**, which contradicts G4's premise that Results
sentences ground a refutation. Cell sizes are 6–15, so this is suggestive rather
than settled.

**What G4 rejects among the 43 G1+G2+G3-passing candidates:**

| rejected group | n | rescues | regressions | precision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| unknown + cross-document | 15 | 9 | 6 | 60.0% |
| methods | 3 | 1 | 2 | 33.3% |
| background | 1 | 1 | 0 | 100% |
| **total rejected** | **19** | **11** | **8** | **57.9%** |

versus what it keeps: results 66.7% (n=6), conclusions 75.0% (n=4), **unknown +
same-document 42.9% (n=14)**.

**A hypothesis, not a finding.** This looks like the same-document fallback
being inverted — G4 rejects a 60%-precision group and keeps a 42.9% one. But at
n=15 versus n=14 that difference is not statistically supported, and over the
*full* 150 overridable claims the marginal ordering runs the designed way
(same-doc 35.7% vs cross-doc 23.2%, n=56 each). So the fallback's direction only
appears to reverse *after conditioning* on G1∧G2∧G3. The defensible statement is
the aggregate one: **G4 removes 19 candidates of which 57.9% were rescues,
slightly above the 55.81% pool rate, costing 3 claims net.** Which component of
G4 is responsible is not resolved by n=504.

## H. Which gates appear useful

**G3 (uncontested support) — strongly justified, and necessary.** Adding it
removes 16 candidates of which only 18.8% were rescues, against a 40.0% pool
rate — by far the sharpest discrimination of the four. All 8 subsets lacking it
are net-negative. Mechanistically clear: when the retrieved premise *entails*
the claim, a contradictory fragment elsewhere in the pool is not evidence the
claim is false.

**G1 (symmetry) — justified, with a caveat.** Rejects 39.1% of counter-evidence
claims, so it is far from inert. In the absence of G4 it is a strong filter
(removes 38.9% good against a 48.10% pool). Its contribution collapses once G4
is present because the two reject overlapping cases.

## I. Which gates appear weak or redundant

**G2 (coherence) — near-inert but harmless.** 93.4% pass rate overall, 90.0% on
overridable claims; in the full-set context it removes just 3 candidates
(1R/2X). The 19 claims it does reject are genuinely incoherent pools, and it is
free (the NLI call is already made for the record). Keep or drop with little
consequence; do not count it as doing work.

**G4 (finding-grounded) — not justified as currently specified.** It is the only
gate that is net harmful: −3 claims, and the only one whose removals are
*enriched* for rescues. Dropping it yields the best subset in the table
(G1+G2+G3, +0.99pp, net +5). Its stated rationale is undercut by §G: it fires as
a provenance test 69% of the time, and `results` — half of its explicit
allow-list — does not beat the base rate.

**G1 and G4 are partially redundant.** Pairwise agreement 58.5% on the 289
identifiable claims, and each one's marginal value disappears in the other's
presence.

## J. Is the four-gate adjudicator justified?

**The mechanism: yes.** Both the shipped conjunction and the best subset beat
the base rate by a wide and significant margin:

| rule | precision | × base rate (29.33%) | expected R if random | observed R | P(X ≥ obs) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1+G2+G3+G4 (shipped) | 54.17% (13/24) | 1.85× | 7.0 | 13 | 0.0094 |
| G1+G2+G3 (best) | 55.81% (24/43) | 1.90× | 12.6 | 24 | **0.00026** |

Gated adjudication is not a volume filter dressed up as discrimination; it
selects substantially better than chance, and the larger-n best subset makes
that case more strongly than the shipped one does.

**This specific conjunction: no.** Three of the four gates are justified
(G3 strongly, G1 moderately, G2 trivially); **G4 is net harmful.** The shipped
rule is 3 claims worse than G1+G2+G3.

**But do not over-read the ranking.** 3 claims out of 504 is well inside noise,
and the best-of-16 selection inflates any single comparison. What survives that
caution is the *consistent* evidence: the 8-vs-0 G3 pattern, G3's 18.8%-vs-40.0%
leave-one-out margin, and G4 being the sole gate whose removals are enriched for
rescues. The headline accuracy ordering within the top six is not reliable.

**Also unchanged:** arm F's overall gain is +0.40pp (shipped) or +0.99pp (best
subset) on n=504, bought with a 3.3–5.7pp SUPPORT loss. Neither is a shippable
improvement yet.

## K. Recommendations for D2 and D3

### D2 (random-k control) — downgrade to optional

D1 answered D2's core question more precisely than D2 would have. The exact base
rate is 29.33% (not estimated), and the binomial tests above (p = 0.0094 shipped,
p = 0.00026 best) already establish that the gates beat random selection. A
random-k run would add an empirical variance estimate and nothing else, at the
cost of a full 504-claim GPU run. **Skip it, or defer it until something needs
the variance.**

### D3 — retarget onto the unknown-section population

The original D3 ("measure whether structured scope features separate rescues
from regressions") was aimed at the wrong population. D1 relocates the problem:

- **112 of the 150 overridable claims (74.7%) have an unknown-section counter**,
  at 29.5% ceiling precision — essentially the base rate. This is where almost
  all the headroom and almost all the damage live.
- The section signal is only informative for `conclusions` (n=10) and
  `background`/`methods` (n=13 combined). Those three cells total 23 claims;
  there is little to gain by refining them.
- G3's success shows the discriminating information is about the **relationship
  between the premise and the claim** (does the source support the claim at
  all?), not about the counter-evidence in isolation.

So **D3 should measure, on the 112 unknown-section overridable claims, what
distinguishes the 33 rescuable from the 79 losable** — and should start with
premise-side and claim-side features, since that is where G3 found signal, not
with counter-evidence section or similarity. Report separation and overlap only;
no criterion, no threshold.

A cheap precursor worth running first, offline on this artifact: check whether
those 112 counters are unknown because the abstracts are genuinely unstructured
or because the heading heuristic missed. §G suggests the former (11/112 sit in
documents whose other spans did carry sections), which would mean section
recovery cannot be improved and the information must come from elsewhere.

## Research conclusion

**Strongest useful gate — G3 (uncontested support).** It is necessary: all 8
subsets without it are net-negative, all 6 net-positive subsets contain it. Its
leave-one-out margin is the widest of the four (removes candidates that are only
18.8% rescues, against a 40.0% pool).

**Weakest / redundant gate — G4 (finding-grounded), with G2 a close second.**
G4 is the only net-harmful gate (−3 claims); dropping it gives the best subset
in the table. It also fires as a provenance test rather than a section test in
69% of cases, and `results` — half its allow-list — does not beat the base rate.
G2 is near-inert (90–93% pass) but harmless and free.

**Does gate ablation change the research direction? Yes, in two ways.** First,
it falsified a prior concern (G1 is not inert — it rejects 39% of cases) and
identified a different culprit (G4). Second, and more consequentially, it
relocated the problem: 74.7% of the overridable population has an
unknown-section counter sitting at base-rate precision, so section- and
counter-centric refinements address at most a quarter of the available signal.
G3's success points at premise-to-claim features instead.

**Proceed to D2? No — skip or defer.** D1 already produced the exact base rate
and significance tests D2 was designed to supply, without a GPU run.

**What D3 should measure.** On the 112 unknown-section overridable claims
(33 rescuable / 79 losable), what distinguishes rescuable from losable — starting
from premise-side and claim-side properties, not counter-evidence section or
embedding similarity (the latter was already ruled out by the scope-alignment
probe). Precede it with the offline check of *why* those sections are unknown.

**Immediate low-cost action available now:** re-run arm F once with G4 disabled
to confirm the G1+G2+G3 subset empirically (it should reproduce 43/24/19 and
84.52% exactly, since D1 predicts it from stored data). That is a validation of
this analysis rather than a new experiment, and it is the only production change
this report would justify.
